# Capacities Believer Plan vs Basic Comparison: Which is Best?

> **Quick Answer:** The Capacities Basic plan offers a generous, unlimited object-based [note-taking](/posts/remnote-vs-logseq-for-medical-students-comparison/) experience ideal for students and casual users. However, power users should upgrade to the Believer plan to unlock the AI Assistant, custom object layouts, advanced task management, and priority support. Choose Basic to learn the tool, and Believer when your [personal knowledge management](/posts/digital-decluttering-for-information-overload/) requires deep automation and custom views.

Personal knowledge management (PKM) has shifted from traditional folder hierarchies to more dynamic, object-based systems. Capacities is at the forefront of this architectural shift, offering a workspace where every note, image, or link is treated as a distinct, typed object. As you evaluate adopting this tool for your daily workflow, the primary decision revolves around its pricing tiers. This Capacities Believer plan vs Basic comparison breaks down the exact differences in features, limitations, and overall value to help you determine which tier aligns with your structural needs.

## The Core Philosophy Behind Capacities

Before comparing the specific tiers, understanding the underlying object-based architecture is essential for making an informed decision. Unlike [Notion](/posts/second-brain-dashboard-for-notion-power-users/)'s fluid block-and-page system or [Obsidian](/posts/personal-knowledge-management-tools-comparison/)'s flat markdown file directories, Capacities requires you to explicitly define what an entry is upon creation—a book, a meeting, a person, or a tweet. This structural rigidity paradoxically creates more freedom later, as databases auto-populate based on object types and relationships are strictly defined. 

Both the Basic and Believer plans utilize this exact same foundational architecture. The difference lies entirely in how you are permitted to manipulate, query, and view these objects. The underlying graph database remains identical, meaning your fundamental approach to note-taking does not need to change if you migrate between tiers.

## Capacities Basic vs Believer: At a Glance

When looking at the two tiers side-by-side, the divergence in their target audiences becomes clear. The Basic tier is designed to remove the friction of adoption, allowing you to map out your initial knowledge graph without hitting restrictive paywalls based on data volume. The Believer tier is positioned as an operating system upgrade, trading a monthly subscription fee for automation, spatial control over your data, and direct access to the development pipeline.

## Key Feature Differences Explored

### AI Integration and Assistance

In the realm of modern digital workspaces, [artificial intelligence](/posts/self-healing-knowledge-base-principles/) serves as a structural necessity for handling large volumes of unstructured text. The Basic tier requires strictly manual data entry for all metadata. If you import a lengthy web article or a PDF report, you must manually read the document, synthesize the summary, and attach the relevant tags or linked objects yourself.

The Believer plan fundamentally alters this intake workflow by integrating advanced language models directly into the editor environment. Capacities AI can automatically analyze the text of an imported object, generate a highly concise summary, and auto-populate metadata properties such as "Topics," "People Mentioned," and "Action Items." Furthermore, the AI Chat function allows you to seamlessly query your own distinct graph. You can ask the prompt interface, "What were the main decisions from last week's marketing syncs?" and the system will synthesize an accurate answer based exclusively on your specific meeting objects. For researchers or analysts dealing with dozens of reference documents weekly, this automated processing saves countless hours of manual data extraction.

### Custom Object Layouts and Properties

Capacities differentiates itself through typed objects, where a 'Book' object inherently possesses different properties than a 'Meeting' object. While the Basic plan permits you to create custom object types and assign basic text, number, or date properties, it locks you into a standard, uniform visual layout for all pages. Every object looks structurally identical.

The Believer plan unlocks custom layouts, giving you complete granular control over the visual hierarchy of your stored data. You can design an overarching dashboard view for a 'Project' object, placing the status property prominently at the very top, followed by a dynamic gallery grid of linked 'Task' objects, and a dedicated narrow sidebar for 'Team Members.' This spatial control transforms Capacities from a simple note repository into a bespoke software application tailored to your exact cognitive architecture. You can easily hide empty properties to reduce visual clutter, pin critical metadata, and choose between list, gallery, or board views for linked databases directly within the page.

### Task Management Capabilities

Knowledge management without integrated action management frequently leads to an archival graveyard where ideas go to die. The Basic plan supports rudimentary markdown checklists within individual pages, but these checklists do not aggregate or surface effectively across your broader workspace. If you write a to-do item deep inside a nested project note, you must rely on your own [memory](/posts/how-to-remember-what-you-read-effectively/) to navigate back to that specific note to check it off.

The Believer plan introduces native, comprehensive task management infrastructure. It elevates the 'Task' to a first-class object that can be queried, filtered, and displayed dynamically anywhere in your environment. You can establish a global command dashboard that pulls all unchecked tasks assigned to the current week, completely regardless of which meeting or project note they originally stemmed from. Additionally, task blocks integrate natively with the daily note timeline, allowing for strict, calendar-based time-blocking and accurate capacity planning.

### Early Access and Development Support

Opting into the Believer tier is, in part, a philosophical decision. Capacities utilizes this tier to directly fund its independent development cycle, actively bypassing the traditional venture capital route that often forces [productivity](/posts/what-is-personal-knowledge-management/) tools to compromise their core vision for rapid, unsustainable user growth. In exchange for this financial support, Believers receive early, exclusive access to alpha and beta features long before they reach the wider Pro or Basic user base. This dynamic means you get to actively shape the product roadmap, provide direct critical feedback to the founding engineering team in private Discord channels, and utilize cutting-edge modules weeks or even months before the public release cycle.

## Performance, Sync, and Offline Access

When evaluating any cloud-centric PKM tool, data ownership, access speed, and reliability are paramount concerns. Both the Basic and Believer tiers operate on the exact same robust backend infrastructure, meaning fundamental sync speed across your desktop and mobile devices remains consistently fast regardless of your subscription status. Capacities utilizes an intelligent [local-first](/posts/anytype-review-local-first-privacy-lovers/) caching system; when you launch the application, your most recent and frequently accessed objects are loaded near-instantly from local device storage, while background network processes handle the heavier cloud synchronization.

However, the Believer plan introduces expanded capabilities for media storage and file attachment limits. If your specific workflow involves uploading high-resolution architectural diagrams, raw audio recordings, or heavy, multi-page PDF dossiers, the Basic plan’s per-file upload caps will quickly become a hard bottleneck. The Believer tier significantly raises these friction points, allowing the application to serve as a comprehensive digital filing cabinet. Currently, maintaining full offline editability for complex, object-based graph databases is an ongoing engineering challenge, but continuous infrastructure improvements to local caching disproportionately benefit Believer users who manage massive, media-heavy databases.

## Practical Advice: How to Decide Which Tier You Need

Transitioning your digital life to a new personal knowledge management system is a significant investment of your time, energy, and cognitive load. Here is practical, scenario-based advice on how to navigate the Capacities pricing tiers based on specific user profiles and data requirements.

If you are a university student, a casual daily journaler, or someone just beginning to explore linked thought systems, stay firmly on the Basic plan. The free tier offers unlimited objects and spaces, which is exceptionally rare in this specific software category. You can build a comprehensive [Zettelkasten](/posts/zettelkasten-method-explained/), track your daily habits meticulously, and link your extensive class notes without ever hitting a hard paywall. Furthermore, the lack of AI features can actually prove beneficial for academic students, as the manual process of reading, summarizing, and tagging text directly improves material retention and deeper comprehension.

If you are a freelancer or independent business owner utilizing Capacities as a lightweight CRM or client project tracker, the Believer plan becomes structurally necessary once your database scales. When you reach a threshold where you actively manage more than fifty client objects or simultaneous projects, the lack of custom layouts in the Basic plan introduces severe visual fatigue. Upgrading allows you to build dense, information-rich dashboards that surface critical client metadata, upcoming deadlines, and outstanding invoices instantly.

If your primary professional workflow involves aggressively processing external information—such as a market researcher, technical writer, or data analyst—the Believer plan should be adopted immediately. The AI-driven auto-tagging, property extraction, and contextual summarization will definitively save you multiple hours per week. The ability to import a lengthy industry report, instruct the AI to extract the core arguments, and automatically link those arguments to your existing, persistent topic objects is a transformative workflow that fundamentally justifies the monthly cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I export my data easily if I downgrade from Believer to Basic?
Yes. Capacities maintains a strict data ownership philosophy. Regardless of your active tier, you can export your entire workspace to standard open formats like Markdown and JSON. Downgrading will lock your access to premium visual layouts and AI tools, but your underlying text content and object links remain fully accessible and exportable.

### Are the AI features in the Believer plan unlimited?
No. To effectively manage API costs with external providers like OpenAI and Anthropic, Capacities imposes fair-use monthly limits on AI graph queries and auto-tagging generation within the Believer plan. However, these baseline limits are calibrated high enough that standard daily usage by a power user will rarely trigger a hard restriction.

### Does the Basic plan have a hard limit on the number of objects I can create?
Unlike many competing platforms that rigidly cap their free tiers at 1,000 individual blocks or pages, the Capacities Basic plan generously offers unlimited objects and workspace spaces. You can meticulously build a massive, interconnected graph over several years without ever being forced to upgrade based solely on your total database size.

### Is full offline mode exclusive to the Believer tier?
Capacities relies heavily on intelligent cloud syncing for its graph architecture, utilizing local caching to prioritize speed. While full offline editing of the entire database is an ongoing development priority rather than a finished feature, foundational local caching is available to all users. Believers often receive early access to deeper offline sync capabilities as they are tested in beta.
