# 7 Best Capacities Templates for University Students in 2026

Managing the influx of information at university requires more than just a digital notebook; it requires a structured system. Over the past few years, Capacities has emerged as a powerhouse for [personal knowledge management](/posts/digital-decluttering-for-information-overload/) (PKM). Unlike traditional folder-based apps, Capacities uses an object-based architecture. You don't put a note in a folder; you create an object—like a "Book," a "Course," or an "Assignment"—and link them together naturally. 

For university students, this architectural difference is a massive advantage. You can link a single "Professor" object to the specific "Course" they teach, the "Assignments" they grade, and the "Meetings" you have during their office hours. The friction of deciding where a note belongs vanishes. 

However, setting up object types, properties, and dashboard views from scratch takes time that most students simply do not have. Templates bypass this initial setup phase, providing pre-configured objects, relations, and page layouts tailored specifically for academic life. ## How to Choose the Right Capacities Setup

Selecting a template is about understanding your primary bottleneck as a student. If your notes are scattered across multiple notebooks, a comprehensive system like the Student OS is the right choice. If you are drowning in PDFs and citations, the Literature Review Matrix will provide the structure you need.

When evaluating a template, examine its object hierarchy. A good Capacities setup minimizes the number of object types. If a template has 20 different object types, it is likely too complex to maintain during finals week. Stick to systems that rely on 4 to 6 core objects (e.g., Course, Assignment, Source, Concept, Person).

Additionally, consider how the template uses properties. Properties in Capacities allow you to filter and sort your database. A robust academic template should include properties like "Semester," "Course Relation," and "Status" (Active/Archived). This ensures that when the fall semester ends, you can easily archive those objects so they don't clutter your workspace in the spring, while still remaining accessible for future reference.

## Maximizing Capacities' Object-Based System for College

To get the most out of these templates, you need to lean into the object-based philosophy. Do not treat Capacities like a standard word processor. 

**Define Your Relationships:**
When you import an assignment template, ensure it is permanently linked to the relevant Course object. When you create a new lecture note, link it to the Professor object. Over the course of a degree, this creates a deeply interconnected graph. If you ever need a letter of recommendation, you can click on the Professor's object and instantly see every lecture note, assignment, and meeting you ever had with them.

**Utilize the Daily Note:**
Your academic life happens chronologically. Use the daily note as your inbox. Instead of navigating to your "Biology 101" dashboard to jot down a quick reminder about a quiz, type it in your daily note and tag the `@Biology 101` course object. Capacities will automatically surface that reminder when you visit the biology page later.

**Leverage Tagging for Concepts:**
While objects represent the "things" in your academic life, tags should represent the thematic "concepts." For example, you might have "Lecture" objects from both a Psychology course and an Economics course that discuss decision-making. By applying a `#behavioral-economics` tag to blocks within both lectures, you create a bridge between the disciplines, which is invaluable for interdisciplinary essays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Capacities free for university students?
Capacities offers a highly functional free tier that covers the core object-based note-taking features, which is sufficient for most undergraduate students. They also offer an educational discount for students and teachers who want to upgrade to Capacities Pro to access advanced features like AI integration and unlimited image hosting.

### How does Capacities compare to Notion for college?
Notion is a page-and-database builder, meaning you have to build your own architecture from scratch, which can be brittle. Capacities is object-based out of the box, meaning it automatically organizes your notes by type (e.g., all "Books" go together automatically). Capacities is generally better for networked thought and research, while Notion excels at highly customized dashboards and collaborative [project management](/posts/tana-supertags-for-project-managers-download/).

### Can I use Capacities offline during lectures?
Capacities has introduced offline support, allowing you to access cached content and write new notes without an internet connection. Once you reconnect to campus Wi-Fi, your changes will automatically sync to the cloud.

### How do I import templates into my Capacities workspace?
Most Capacities templates are shared via a public link. Once you click the link, you will see a "Duplicate" or "Add to my workspace" button in the top right corner. Clicking this will clone the object types, properties, and layouts directly into your personal account. 

### Does Capacities integrate with reference managers like Zotero?
Currently, Capacities does not have a native, two-way sync with Zotero. However, you can export Zotero annotations as Markdown and paste them into Capacities, or utilize community-built webhooks to push new Zotero entries into Capacities as "Source" objects automatically.
